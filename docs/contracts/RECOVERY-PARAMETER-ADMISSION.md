# Recovery scenario admission decision

Reviewed 2026-10-16. R11/R12 remain incomplete; this decision activates no public physiological model.

## What is missing

The existing conditional model requires a complete named numerical hypothesis: initial state and scale, fatigue accrual, fast/persistent split, intensity response, recovery rate, and the meaning of service versus rest exposure. Units, route/control mapping, output interpretation and supported duration must agree with that hypothesis. A checksum confirms artifact integrity, not scientific validity.

An explicitly hypothetical comparison need not be personally calibrated. However, changing six unknown coefficients into six friendly questions does not create evidence. The current source review produced no complete supported hypothesis for this law. Synthetic tests establish implementation behavior only.

## Primary-source findings

- [Vassallo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-019-04266-8) studies above-critical running work/recovery, not an observation map from this product's route-effort/log-pace state to stationary trail aid-station recovery.
- [Vaccari et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-022-05059-2) likewise concerns high-intensity running recovery; the published protocol does not establish ultra-distance station exposure or later pace benefits.
- [Jaszczak and Płociniczak, author preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02919v1) supplies a different force/energy/nutrition law. Its fatigue equation has no stop-decay term and its duration correction explicitly excludes times above 940 minutes. Route-record time enters the correction before comparison with those records, so those comparisons are not held-out prediction evidence. The [2025 publication record](https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1629936) confirms the paper; equation-level statements here concern the inspected preprint, not an assumed text-identical final paper.

## Station exposure is not just clock arithmetic

The current sequential assumption uses exposure `rho × service + extra rest`. The containing-service assumption uses the entire dwell as exposure. Overlap in clock time does not prove that either is a physiological recovery dose. Sequential service-only observations without pure-rest contrast identify at most a recovery-rate/exposure product; they do not separately establish both quantities.

## Reopening criteria

Admit a scenario only after its complete parameter set, reference state, control-to-effort map, service/rest exposure, output meaning and domain are independently reviewed. Keep hypothetical results visibly distinct from calibrated predictions. Personal best-rest claims additionally require appropriate real observations and validation of the intervention being recommended. A new model family must be justified separately, not inserted as unrelated coefficients in the current law.

Existing manual pace, station time accounting and explicit stop comparisons remain available. They do not claim physiological recovery or optimal rest.
